## Purpose and requirements

Protect the board from the 48 V input, measure the input current, and generate the low-voltage rails every other sheet runs on (`+5V`, `+3V3`), from either the 48 V bus or an external 5 V bench supply.

Targets are in [specifications.md](../specifications.md); boundaries and interface signals are in the **Input and supply** section of [schematic-hierarchy.md](../schematic-hierarchy.md).

**Requirements**

- Accept the full input voltage range in the specifications: 40–51 V, 48 V nominal (decision #2).
- Carry the input current: 3.95 A at 150 W and 40 V, with parts sized for the 200 W headroom (5.3 A).
- Clamp input transients below the switch-node limit published by power_stage: **90 V** (the LM5113-Q1's HS rating), including ringing. Choose the TVS clamp voltage, the fuse rating and that limit together; GaN has essentially no avalanche capability, so the clamp must stop a transient before it gets there.
- Target an ordinary hot-plug below the TVS's breakdown voltage, so the TVS is reserved for faults. This target is **unverified**: the revised no-TVS model reaches about 57.4 V for one lead assumption, above the 56.7 V minimum breakdown specification.
- Protect against reverse polarity on both J201 (48 V) and J202 (5 V).
- Supply about 150 mA on `+5V` (GaN driver bias, 3.3 V LDO, USB bridge, LEDs) from an aux regulator rated for at least 300 mA.
- Select automatically between J202 and the aux regulator, with reverse-current blocking. Favor a healthy aux supply after it starts while retaining J202-only bring-up; verify that the `+5V` rail rides through every source change without resetting the MCU. If the MCU does reset, the enable sheet holds both gates off.
- Set the mux crossover so aux can take over while J202 still supplies a healthy 5 V, subject to comparator and resistor tolerances (decision #28).
- Rate J202 for at least 0.5 A. Overcurrent on the 5 V path is limited by the bench supply and the mux's current limit (decision #15).
- Provide `+3V3` for the MCU (VDDIO and VDDA, decision #19) and logic, regulating through the `+5V` hand-over dip (input down to ≈ 4.4 V).
- Allow full low-voltage bring-up from J202 with no 48 V connected.
- Measure the board's input current with a high-side shunt (decision #17), so firmware can report input power, estimate efficiency and limit input current.

**Status (2026-11-10):** schematic complete, including the indicator LEDs, and `I_SH_IN_P` / `I_SH_IN_N` are wired to sensing on the root sheet. The sheet was re-annotated on 2026-11-06 (commit `e85a5c7`); references throughout this note follow the new annotation.

## Layout notes

What the layout owner must know: loops to keep small, parts to place first, keep-outs, thermal needs.

- **D201 right at J201**, with short, wide connections to both terminals. Every nanohenry between them adds L·di/dt on top of the 82.4 V clamp and spends the 7.6 V margin.
- **`V_IN_RTN` is its own copper.** It runs from J201− to D201 and Q201's drain pads only; it reaches GND only through Q201. A stray via or pour that touches GND bypasses the reverse-polarity protection.
- Route D202's anode straight to Q201's source pad, not into the general ground pour, so the zener clamps the FET's actual gate-source voltage.
- Q201's drain pads and exposed pad carry the full input current: pour copper on them for current and heat.
- J201 and J301 are identical terminals. Put them on **opposite board edges**, with large `48 V IN  + / −` and `12 V OUT  + / −` silkscreen, and give both the same polarity orientation. J202 can sit anywhere convenient: being keyed, it can't be confused with them.
- Place C203 between Q201 and the power stage, not inside the power loop. The trace inductance between C203 and the power-stage ceramics keeps switching ripple out of C203 (the ripple estimate assumes about 20 nH).
- Size `V_IN`, `V_IN_RTN` and GND copper for 5.3 A (200 W at 40 V).
- **R201 (shunt):** in the high-side path after the front end, before C203 and U201's tap. Place each net tie (NT201, NT202) against the **inner edge** of its shunt pad, and route `I_SH_IN_P` / `I_SH_IN_N` as a tight pair from there to the sensing amplifier, away from the switch node.
- **U201 (LM5164):** Place C206, C207 and C213–C215 as one local bank across VIN (2) and GND (1), with the shortest two parts immediately at the pins and the other three closely beside them. All five need a short return to the same local GND copper to count as high-frequency input capacitance. Keep SW (8) short to L201; put R206, R208 and C204 at FB (5); route the Type-3 network and FB away from SW except R205's own end; tie the exposed pad to GND copper.
- **U203 (TPS2121):** C210 and C211 as close to IN1 and OUT as possible; wide traces for IN1, IN2 and OUT. Keep the dividers short to PR1, OV1 and CP2.
- **U202 (LDL1117):** the tab is **VOUT**, not GND. Give it a copper pour on the `+3V3` net for heat, kept clear of ground copper. C208 at pin 3, C209 at pin 2.
- **Test points:** TP203 (GND) right beside TP201, TP202, TP204 and TP205, so a scope probe's ground spring reaches.
- **48V LED (LED201):** beside J201, without pushing D201 away from J201 (indicator-leds.md). LED201's cathode goes to GND, never to `V_IN_RTN`.

## Grounding and scope measurements

The present Q201 interrupts the input return between J201− (`V_IN_RTN`) and board `GND`. With correct polarity, an earth-bonded supply negative and an earthed scope ground clip on board `GND` form a parallel return around Q201; USB, JTAG and grounded loads can do the same. Use a supply whose **DC output floats from earth** for the prototype, while keeping instrument chassis protective earth connected. Attach ordinary scope ground clips only to board `GND`, never J201−. Reversing the leads alone does not create the same bypass: supply negative then lands on J201+, and Q201 blocks J201−. A clip on J201− would instead contact supply positive. [Tektronix explains the probe-ground earth connection and ground-loop current](https://www.tek.com/en/documents/whitepaper/abcs-probes-primer).

Moving reverse protection to the positive rail and directly joining J201− to board `GND` is **not a complete grounded-scope fix**. It removes the low-side FET from the return, but an earth-bonded supply negative and scope still provide parallel return paths, so some current can flow through the probe ground lead. Worse, if the input leads are reversed with the grounded scope attached, supply positive lands on J201− and can flow through board `GND`, the scope lead and earth to supply negative. That short bypasses F201, which is on J201+. A high-side-only replacement must not be presented as safe for that hookup.

Board-level alternatives need a new design: an isolated measurement output can keep a scope earth connection away from board `GND` for selected signals, subject to bandwidth and fault-rating checks; a coordinated two-pole input disconnect can isolate J201− during reversed hookup, but does not by itself remove the normal-operation earth loop. Neither is implemented. Retain the floating-output bench setup until the complete input and probing arrangement is reviewed.
